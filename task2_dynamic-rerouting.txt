 TASK 2 — DYNAMIC REROUTING : RESULTS & IMPLEMENTATION REPORT

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 0.  EXPERIMENT SETUP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Synthetic dataset (identical to run_demo.py):
     generate(n_aisles=10, n_positions=20, n_items=300,
              n_pickruns=500, n_days=1, seed=42)

     - 200 locations (10 aisles x 20 positions)
     - 500 pickruns, avg 4.8 items/run
     - One-way aisles at x in {1, 3, 5, 7, 9} (odd columns, northbound only)

 Routes: nearest-neighbor TSP (solver="nn").

 IMPORTANT — releases synchronised to t=0 for all contention experiments.
     The raw transaction timestamps stagger pickrun releases across a ~16 h
     day. With staggered releases the makespan is RELEASE-BOUND: the last
     pickrun cannot start until it is released, so contention and rerouting
     have negligible effect (makespan stays ~958 min regardless of settings).
     Rerouting only matters when pickers compete for one-way aisles at the
     SAME time, so every contention experiment below releases all pickruns at
     t=0. This is what makes the threshold sweep meaningful.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 IMPORTANT FIX — the original reroute heuristic ignored its own threshold
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 The original reroute branch only tested `len(aisle_res[x].queue) > 0`, so it
 rerouted IMMEDIATELY on any queue and never measured how long the picker had
 waited. `reroute_on_wait_s` was therefore just an on/off switch — thresholds
 of 5 s and 60 s produced byte-identical results, making the 2a sweep flat and
 meaningless.

 Fixed in des.py: the picker now uses SimPy's reneging pattern
     got = req in (yield req | env.timeout(reroute_on_wait_s))
 It genuinely waits up to the threshold and only gives up (and re-solves) if it
 still has not acquired the aisle AND has other tiles it could pick meanwhile.
 The threshold now governs behaviour, as the tables below show.


================================================================================
 2a.  THRESHOLD SENSITIVITY
================================================================================
 Question : Does rerouting help more when contention is high (many pickers)
            or low? Sweep reroute_on_wait_s in {5,10,20,30,60} at 2/3/5 pickers.

 Plot     : task2a_threshold_sensitivity.png  (3 panels)
              Panel 1 — absolute makespan vs threshold, one line per picker count
              Panel 2 — % improvement over the no-rerouting baseline
              Panel 3 — total reroutes triggered (bar chart)

 Results (makespan in minutes; % = improvement vs that picker count's baseline;
          reroutes = total renege/re-solve events over the whole run):

  n_pickers = 2   (baseline makespan = 609.24 min)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
   threshold(s)  |  makespan(min)  |   vs base   |   reroutes
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
        5        |     607.02      |   +0.36%    |      41
       10        |     608.94      |   +0.05%    |      39
       20        |     610.49      |   -0.20%    |      25
       30        |     608.39      |   +0.14%    |      10
       60        |     609.11      |   +0.02%    |       2
  ----------------------------------------------------------------

  n_pickers = 3   (baseline makespan = 419.64 min)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
   threshold(s)  |  makespan(min)  |   vs base   |   reroutes
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
        5        |     414.43      |   +1.24%    |     124
       10        |     415.73      |   +0.93%    |      99
       20        |     418.11      |   +0.37%    |      64
       30        |     417.69      |   +0.47%    |      36
       60        |     418.87      |   +0.19%    |       6
  ----------------------------------------------------------------

  n_pickers = 5   (baseline makespan = 269.16 min)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
   threshold(s)  |  makespan(min)  |   vs base   |   reroutes
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
        5        |     267.63      |   +0.57%    |     317
       10        |     267.00      |   +0.80%    |     191
       20        |     268.18      |   +0.36%    |     132
       30        |     267.78      |   +0.51%    |      88
       60        |     269.00      |   +0.06%    |      20
  ----------------------------------------------------------------

 FINDINGS
   * Rerouting helps MORE when contention is high. At 5 pickers it fires 317
     times (threshold 5 s) vs 41 at 2 pickers; the 3- and 5-picker curves sit
     well above the 2-picker curve on the % improvement panel.
   * Lower thresholds reroute more aggressively and generally help more, with
     diminishing returns past ~20 s. Reroute count falls off monotonically as
     the threshold rises (e.g. 3 pickers: 124 -> 99 -> 64 -> 36 -> 6).
   * The absolute payoff is honestly small (sub-1.5% makespan). One-way-aisle
     waits are a minor slice of total pick work even under full contention, so
     dodging them only trims a little off the makespan.
   * Rerouting can BACKFIRE: at 2 pickers / 20 s the makespan went slightly
     negative (-0.20%) — a picker took a longer detour to dodge a queue that
     would have cleared quickly. This is a good argument for tuning the
     threshold rather than setting it aggressively low.


================================================================================
 2b.  RE-SOLVE INSTEAD OF DEFER
================================================================================
 Change   : Instead of a plain list reorder, the reroute branch now re-runs the
            TSP solver on the remaining un-blocked tiles and re-attaches the
            blocked aisle at the end, via tsp._apply_solver(reroute_solver, ...).
            New parameter: reroute_solver (default "nn"); any solver accepted by
            _apply_solver is valid ("nn", "2opt", "or_opt", "sa", ...).

 Results (3 pickers, synchronised releases; baseline makespan = 419.64 min):

  ------------------------------------------------------------------
   reroute_solver | threshold(s) | makespan(min) |  vs base | reroutes
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
   nn             |      5       |    414.43     |  +1.24%  |   124
   nn             |     10       |    415.73     |  +0.93%  |    99
   nn             |     30       |    417.69     |  +0.47%  |    36
   2opt           |      5       |    410.62     |  +2.15%  |   111
   2opt           |     10       |    413.88     |  +1.37%  |    93
   2opt           |     30       |    417.12     |  +0.60%  |    36
   or_opt         |      5       |    410.78     |  +2.11%  |   107
   or_opt         |     10       |    412.76     |  +1.64%  |    93
   or_opt         |     30       |    415.87     |  +0.90%  |    31
  ------------------------------------------------------------------

 FINDINGS
   * Re-solving with a better local-search solver (2opt / or_opt) roughly
     DOUBLES the gain over plain nn re-solve at the same threshold
     (e.g. threshold 5 s: nn +1.24% -> 2opt +2.15%), because the re-attached
     route over the remaining tiles is itself shorter.
   * 2opt and or_opt are close to each other; or_opt edges ahead at the
     mid-range threshold (10 s).
   * The cost is solver runtime per reroute event — acceptable here because
     the per-run tile counts are small (avg ~5 picks/run).


================================================================================
 2c.  COORDINATED DISPATCH
================================================================================
 Change   : New parameter coordinated_dispatch=True replaces per-pickrun
            pre-assigned routes with a shared work-pool (a flat list of every
            pick tile from all pickruns). Exactly n_pickers worker processes
            draw the nearest available tile from the pool until it is empty,
            batching the full aisle on each one-way-aisle visit. A central
            dispatcher process wakes every 10 s, inspects aisle queues, and
            pushes tiles in any congested aisle to the back of the pool to
            balance load. Makespan is taken from the last picker's actual
            finish time (max of per-picker finish), not env.now.

 Results (synchronised releases):

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   n_pickers | baseline(min) | reroute@10s(min) | coordinated(min) | coord gain
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
       2     |    609.24     |     608.94        |      89.27       |   -85.3%
       3     |    419.64     |     415.73        |      56.24       |   -86.6%
       5     |    269.16     |      267.00       |      34.78       |   -87.1%
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   (coord gain = makespan reduction vs the per-pickrun baseline)

 In the coordinated runs the dispatcher recorded 0 reroutes and 0 s average
 aisle wait at every picker count — the continuous nearest-tile sweep naturally
 fans pickers out so they rarely collide on a one-way aisle in this dataset.

 FINDINGS
   * Coordinated dispatch is dramatically faster (~85-87% lower makespan) than
     both the baseline and per-pickrun rerouting.
   * CAVEAT — this is NOT a like-for-like comparison. The per-pickrun model
     makes each picker complete a full pickrun (depot -> picks -> depot) before
     taking the next, so it pays a depot round-trip per run. The coordinated
     model does one continuous warehouse sweep with no per-run depot returns.
     Most of the gain comes from eliminating those depot round-trips, not from
     load-balancing alone. The two models answer different operational
     questions (discrete cart-based pickruns vs continuous zone-free picking).
   * Because contention never actually built up (0 reroutes), the dispatcher's
     load-balancing did not fire on this dataset; its value would show on
     denser layouts or with longer one-way aisles where collisions are common.
