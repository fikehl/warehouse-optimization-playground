 0.  EXPERIMENT SETUP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Synthetic dataset (identical to run_demo.py):
     generate(n_aisles=10, n_positions=20, n_items=300,
              n_pickruns=500, n_days=1, seed=42)

     - 200 locations (10 aisles x 20 positions)
     - 500 pickruns, avg 4.8 items/run
     - Zone layout (default): aisles grouped by temperature, ambient first.
         AMBIENT  = aisles 1-7   (70%)
         CHILLER  = aisles 8-9   (20%)
         FREEZER  = aisle  10    (10%)
     - cold_last=True forces AMBIENT -> CHILLER -> FREEZER pick ordering, on top
       of the heavy -> normal -> fragile weight ordering (up to 9 segments).

 Cold-chain background:
     A "cold" item is one whose location zone is CHILLER or FREEZER. The point
     of cold_last is to pick cold items as late as possible so they spend the
     least time out of refrigeration sitting in the cart.

 DES note (4c/4d): releases synchronised to t=0 (SYNC_RELEASES), as in
 task2/task3 — otherwise makespan is release-bound and per-crossing penalties
 are swamped by the 16 h release window.

 Reproduce:
     python task4_cold_chain.py        # 4a / 4b / 4c / 4d, all sections


================================================================================
 4a.  HOW OFTEN DOES THE RAW ORDER VIOLATE COLD-CHAIN?
================================================================================
 Question : Compare the ordering_fixes column with and without cold_last. Each
            EXTRA fix is a cold item the raw order would have picked before an
            ambient/normal one.

 ordering_fixes counts how many items arrive out of the enforced (zone, weight)
 order in the raw transaction sequence:
   - without cold_last : weight axis only (heavy->normal->fragile)
   - with cold_last    : weight axis PLUS zone axis (ambient->chiller->freezer)
 so the difference isolates pure cold-chain violations.

 Run the SAME full solver comparison as run_demo.py [3/6] (200-pickrun sample)
 and read the ordering_fixes column down the cold-last vs non-cold-last rows.

 Results (avg ordering_fixes per pickrun, 200-pickrun sample, seed=42):

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   solver   | fixes (weight only) | fixes (+cold-chain) | extra (cold-chain)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   nn       |        0.55         |        1.18         |      +0.62
   2opt     |        0.55         |        1.18         |      +0.62
   or_opt   |        0.55         |        1.18         |      +0.62
   aisle_nn |        0.55         |        1.18         |      +0.62
   bucketed |        0.55         |        1.18         |      +0.62
   sa       |        0.55         |        1.18         |      +0.62
   mst      |        0.55         |        1.18         |      +0.62
   aco      |        0.55         |        1.18         |      +0.62
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   (over all 500 pickruns the numbers are 0.53 / 1.20 / +0.66.)

 FINDINGS
   * ordering_fixes is IDENTICAL for every solver. It is computed from the raw
     transaction order BEFORE any solver runs (tsp.route_pickrun), so it is a
     property of the input data, not of the route the solver builds. Running
     all eight solvers makes this invariance explicit.
   * The raw order commits ~0.62 cold-chain violations per pickrun on average —
     i.e. in a meaningful fraction of orders a CHILLER/FREEZER item appears
     before an ambient/normal item it should follow.
   * Total weight+cold violations more than double (0.55 -> 1.18) once the cold
     axis is enforced, confirming the raw picking sequence frequently ignores
     temperature ordering. The segmented router then re-orders to satisfy it.


================================================================================
 4b.  COST OF THE CONSTRAINT  (grouped vs interspersed cold zones)
================================================================================
 Change   : data_gen.generate() gained random_zones (default False). When True,
            every location is assigned a zone independently at random (same
            70/20/10 split) instead of by aisle, so cold slots are interspersed
            across all aisles rather than clustered in one block.
            (Only consumes rng in the True branch, so the default seed=42 stream
            — and therefore every existing result — is unchanged.)

 Question : enforcing cold-last often REDUCES distance when cold zones are
            grouped (you stop backtracking into the cold block). Does it cost
            distance when zones are interspersed?

 Results (avg routed distance/run, solver=nn, seed=42):

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   layout                | base dist | cold-last dist | penalty vs base
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   grouped (default)     |  185.0 m  |    180.9 m     |    -2.2%
   interspersed          |  189.6 m  |    209.8 m     |   +10.6%
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------

 FINDINGS
   * Grouped layout: cold-last is actually CHEAPER (-2.2%). Because the cold
     aisles are one contiguous block, picking them last clusters the route
     spatially and removes backtracking into the cold area.
   * Interspersed layout: cold-last costs +10.6%. With cold slots scattered
     everywhere, forcing "all ambient, then all chiller, then all freezer"
     makes the picker zig-zag across the whole warehouse three times.
   * CONCLUSION: the cold-last constraint is close to free (often beneficial)
     in a realistic zone-grouped warehouse, but carries a real distance penalty
     in a poorly-slotted interspersed one. Slotting layout and the cold-chain
     rule interact — good zone grouping makes compliance cheap.


================================================================================
 4c.  DWELL-TIME MODEL IN THE DES
================================================================================
 Change   : run_des() gained zone_lookup ({tile: zone}). When supplied the DES
            tracks, for every CHILLER/FREEZER pick, the "dwell time" = seconds
            from when the cold item is picked until its pickrun finishes (it
            rides in the cart that whole time). New return keys:
               avg_cold_dwell_s, max_cold_dwell_s, total_cold_dwell_s,
               n_cold_picks
            Implemented in _do_route via a per-pickrun list of cold-pick
            timestamps, differenced against the pickrun end time.

 Why dwell beats ordering violations: even a perfectly ordered route keeps the
 FIRST chiller item in the cart for the entire freezer segment, so dwell
 measures actual exposure that an ordering-only check misses.

 Results (3 pickers, synchronised releases, default grouped layout):

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   routes        | avg cold dwell | max cold dwell | cold picks
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   plain NN      |    104.3 s     |    338.7 s     |    678
   cold-last NN  |     32.7 s     |    138.7 s     |    678
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------

 FINDINGS
   * cold-last cuts average cold-item dwell by ~69% (104.3 s -> 32.7 s) and the
     worst-case dwell by ~59% (338.7 s -> 138.7 s).
   * Same number of cold picks (678) in both — the ordering, not the pick set,
     changes. cold-last simply moves those picks to the end of each run so they
     spend far less time warming in the cart.
   * This quantifies the cold-chain benefit in TIME (refrigeration exposure),
     which is the operationally meaningful metric, rather than just counting
     ordering violations as in 4a.


================================================================================
 4d.  ZONE-CROSSING PENALTY  (does it change which solver wins?)
================================================================================
 Change   : run_des(zone_cross_penalty_s=s) adds env.timeout(s) whenever two
            consecutive PICKED tiles belong to different zones (handling/door
            overhead between temperature areas). Requires zone_lookup. New
            return key n_zone_crossings counts the penalised transitions.

 Question : does the penalty change which routing wins?

 Results (3 pickers, 60 s per zone crossing, synchronised releases):

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   routing        | makespan (no penalty) | makespan (+penalty) | crossings
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   nn             |       419.6 min       |      697.0 min      |    766
   2opt           |       401.3 min       |      660.2 min      |    733
   aisle_nn       |       428.8 min       |      651.8 min      |    653
   nn cold-last   |       412.6 min       |      572.7 min      |    472
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------

 FINDINGS
   * YES — the winner flips. With no penalty, plain 2opt wins on makespan
     (401.3 min) and cold-last is only mid-pack (412.6 min).
   * With a steep 60 s per-crossing penalty, nn cold-last wins decisively
     (572.7 min) — it crosses the fewest zone boundaries (472 vs 733 for 2opt)
     because it groups all picks by zone, so it pays the least penalty.
   * aisle_nn (S-shape sweep) also climbs the ranking (653 crossings) for the
     same reason — sweeping by aisle column keeps same-zone tiles together.
   * CONCLUSION: when crossing temperature boundaries is expensive, the solver
     that minimises raw walking distance is NOT the best choice; a zone-aware
     ordering (cold_last / aisle sweep) wins by minimising crossings. The right
     objective depends on whether distance or zone-crossing dominates the cost.
